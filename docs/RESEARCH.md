# Fanam name story

Research checked on 31 October 2026.

## Product naming evidence

`src/lib/brand.js` in the supplied app says Fanio takes its name from the historic Fanam. `src/components/Brand/BrandStory.jsx` renders that brand story and the motto. The website therefore preserves the app's documented naming inspiration; it does not invent an explanation for the suffix “-io”.

## Historical evidence

| Source | What it supports |
| --- | --- |
| [Merriam-Webster — fanam](https://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/fanam) | Fanam refers to a small gold or silver coin formerly used widely in southern India. |
| [Museums Victoria — Coin, 1 Fanam, Travancore, India, 1911](https://collections.museumsvictoria.com.au/items/64523) | A cataloged silver fanam issued in Travancore supplies a concrete historical example. |

The page paraphrases this material. Its claim is deliberately limited to small gold or silver coins used in southern India and surviving examples from Travancore. It does not assert one fixed exchange value, a universal coin material, an exclusive Kerala origin, or a definitive first issue date.

The dictionary gives a qualified etymology, describing a probable connection with Tamil *panam*. The landing page omits that uncertain linguistic claim because it is not necessary to tell the brand story accurately.

## Website copy

“Our name begins with Fanam: a small gold or silver coin historically used in southern India. Museum collections preserve examples from Travancore, part of the region’s rich monetary history.”

The following paragraph connects that history with everyday budgeting. That is a modern brand interpretation, grounded in the supplied product's positioning, rather than an assertion about the historical meaning of the coin.

The image displayed beside the story is the app's original `fanio-symbol.svg`, identified as Fanio's supplied Fanam-inspired brand artwork. It is not labeled as an archaeological photograph or a specific museum object.

Both source links appear in the page's “Explore the history” disclosure. No remote images are embedded and no source photography is redistributed.
